# Build understanding

Use **perceptual scaffolding**: arrange representations, interaction and feedback
so a person can understand relationships, predict changes and act confidently
with minimal unnecessary mental reconstruction. Calibrate confidence to evidence.
Optimize accurate understanding and control per unit effort; fewer words or more
sensory channels are useful only when they serve that outcome.

## Start with the person's task

Establish what the person needs to understand or accomplish, their relevant
starting knowledge, and the likely costly misunderstanding. Recover available
context before asking questions. Distinguish learning a concept, choosing an
action, exploring possibilities and supplying information; they can need different
representations. Keep the intended outcome visible while choosing the format.

## Make the needed relationships available

Choose the smallest useful representation. Put related information together and
precise labels beside their referents. Use layout, comparison, containment and
patterns to expose relationships that prose would make the reader reconstruct.
Use familiar symbols with labels; teach a new visual vocabulary only when its
benefit justifies learning it. Color reinforces another cue.

Match channels to the person, task, context and available capabilities. Consider
space and visual patterns, precise text, sequence and motion, audio and speech,
touch or haptics, physical action, and human input and feedback. Text can be the
best choice. Read [patterns and evidence](references/patterns-and-evidence.md)
when selecting a medium, designing an explorable or checking the research basis.

Give an unfamiliar subject a useful overview and a concrete worked case. Let
the person reach deeper detail where it becomes relevant. Use before/after,
aligned alternatives or a user-controlled sequence when change matters. Keep
important states available for inspection; a missed animation must not erase
essential meaning. Offer interaction when changing an assumption and seeing
the consequence teaches more than another paragraph. These are choices, not a
required sequence for every response.

## Support understanding in both directions

Make it easy for the person to indicate intent, compare options, try an action
and correct a misunderstanding. Keep choices and their consequences visible
together. Preserve focus, control, reversibility and an understandable return
path. Use the existing input tools and permission boundaries; a useful diagram
does not replace required consent or a precise instruction.

Preserve exact quantities, conditions, exceptions and uncertainty. Provide
equivalent accessible routes, labels and text alternatives. Respect motion and
audio preferences. Do not assume a device, output capability or sensory channel
is available. Choose a representation that works in the actual reader.

For consequential visual UI implementation, use
$opl:design-perceptual-interfaces and its existing channel, motion and observation
guidance. This skill owns the learning/communication purpose; that skill owns
the rendered visual design. For shared submissions and human contributions,
use $opl:human-collaboration's existing brief and lifecycle protocols. Its phone

does not require starting a service or creating an inbox.

## Observe and refine proportionately

Use actual interpretations, predictions, actions and corrections when available.
Check the misunderstanding most likely to change the person's next decision.
An optional worked example can reveal a gap without a quiz or acknowledgement
ritual. Do not require a human test after every reply.

Separate semantic correctness, rendered legibility, human comprehension and
preference. Agent inspection does not establish that the person understands or
learns faster. Report the evidence and its limits. Keep useful corrections with
their existing project context; add no scoring machinery, recurring evaluations
or new memory store. Stop when the scoped communication need is met.
