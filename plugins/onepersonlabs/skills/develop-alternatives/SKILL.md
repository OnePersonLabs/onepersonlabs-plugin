# Develop competing approaches

Turn a consequential unresolved design choice into concrete alternatives that a
project can compare. Develop mechanisms, not labels. Use this skill when research
has left multiple plausible ways to meet a defined goal and choosing the wrong one
would make later work costly. Do not make a competition out of a routine choice or
reopen a settled decision without evidence that its premise changed.

The result is an approach set for `$onepersonlabs:pov`. It does not authorize
implementation or select a project commitment. The calling workflow owns the
result and resumes only after the decision has an owner.

## Establish a fair brief

Recover the requested outcome, constraints, settled decisions, unresolved goals,
project evidence, and the cost of choosing incorrectly. Read applicable accepted
Conceptor meaning and project instructions. Use `$onepersonlabs:research-solutions`
when current implementations, external constraints, or technical integration
facts could change which approaches are viable. Reuse relevant research instead
of repeating it. Load applicable [project knowledge](../agent-instructions/references/project-knowledge.md)
and preserve citations.

Separate known requirements from assumptions. Define comparison criteria from
the user's request and verified project constraints before developing candidates.
Include incumbent and no-change options when they are credible. Keep uncertainty
in the shared brief; candidate-specific assumptions stay with that candidate.

If the real goal or a material requirement is unsettled, return that question to
the existing product owner for resolution before comparing designs. Return open
opportunity fields that need invention to the current workflow owner for ideation.
If the supplied approaches already contain enough mechanism and tradeoff detail,
skip generation and route them to `$onepersonlabs:pov`. Never invent extra candidates
to make the comparison look complete.

## Develop independent candidates

Produce at least two meaningfully distinct, usable approaches under the same
brief. Give each the same substantive requirements. Describe its mechanism, scope,
ownership and state boundaries, dependencies, and fit with the incumbent. Include
failure and recovery behavior, plus the evidence needed to trust the approach.
Make each candidate concrete enough that a reviewer can identify how the result
would differ in a real project case. Do not promote an approach through detail
while giving alternatives only slogans.

Use available native delegation for independent development when it improves
the comparison and fits the caller's authority and resource budget. Give each
candidate the common brief and relevant source evidence, without sibling results
or a preferred answer. One worker may not present multiple candidates as independent
work. If independent routes are unavailable, keep the candidates distinct through
their mechanisms and assumptions, disclose that they share one author, and do not
claim independent development.

Do not request a standing agent fleet, install a CLI, or create new dispatch
infrastructure. Keep discussion and scratch records in the existing project
checkpoint; avoid another plan or decision store.

## Compare and hand back

Check that each candidate addresses the same goal and hard constraints. Identify
where they differ in ownership, dependencies, complexity, reversibility, failure
behavior, and implementation path. Preserve convergent candidates when their
mechanisms differ in a way that matters. Reject only on a verified constraint or
explain why evidence leaves that option unviable; resemblance or popularity is
not a reason by itself.

Route the complete developed set and common criteria to `$onepersonlabs:pov` for a
position grounded in the project. Give an independent reviewer the same brief and
all candidates without your ranking when a high-cost decision needs that check;
return their assessment as evidence, not a vote. A reviewer who authored a
candidate is not independent. Reconcile any decision-changing dispute against
source evidence. Do not require a multi-agent panel for routine design work.

Runtime behavior and subjective human experience need their real evidence paths.
Use `$onepersonlabs:solve-novel-software` when a technical claim could change the
choice. Use `$onepersonlabs:design-perceptual-interfaces` or `$onepersonlabs:prototype`
when experience must settle it. A sketch does not prove runtime behavior. A
model's preference does not prove human preference.

Return the common brief and criteria, at least two candidate mechanisms, their
assumptions and tradeoffs, what they share, material exclusions, source evidence,
open technical or human evidence, whether development was independent, and the
next owner: `$onepersonlabs:pov`. When one candidate alone satisfies the verified constraints, pass that result
and the exclusions to POV. When evidence cannot distinguish candidates or
generation is interrupted, report the unresolved comparison instead of inventing
a winner. The project decides only after its own position and
required human decisions are resolved.
