# Evidence for clear, useful writing

These sources support the shared drafting foundation and its guidance for chat,
UI and pages, documentation, and reports. They supplement the maintained Wikipedia
signs catalogue; this review does not refresh that catalogue or its review dates.

- Steven Pinker, [The Source of Bad Writing](https://stevenpinker.com/files/pinker/files/documents/the-source-of-bad-writing.pdf), September 25, 2014. Writers can forget which terms, steps, and relationships readers do not know. Explaining the missing concrete facts and getting feedback from intended readers can expose that gap. Merely intending to be clear does not prove comprehension.
- GOV.UK, [Clear language](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/). Use familiar language, explain necessary specialist terms, and replace vague jargon with what is actually happening. Clear wording must still express requirements accurately. Its government-specific legal wording and contraction rules do not become universal rules for this plugin.
- Hoa Loranger, NN/g, [Plain Language Is for Everyone, Even Experts](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/plain-language-experts/), October 8, 2017. Expert readers also benefit from concise, scannable prose and informative headings. Familiar specialist terms can improve precision; internal branding and unfamiliar jargon can obstruct understanding. Match explanations to the intended reader rather than defining every technical term.

## Apply the evidence in context

The local guidance combines these findings: start from the reader's goal, explain
concrete functionality before unfamiliar names, make headings informative, remove
nonessential content, and preserve the limits that make claims accurate. The
examples in write-technical/references/concise-responses.md are local illustrations, not quotations or source findings.
No fixed sentence length, reading grade, or word-reduction quota is imposed.

An empty heading, an unexplained internal name, or an omitted limit fails because
of its relationship to the surrounding text and the reader's task. A phrase can
be appropriate in another context. Review the whole artifact for those failures;
do not turn them into keyword bans or add scanner rules. Mechanical checks remain
advisory and cannot establish usability, factual completeness, or comprehension.
