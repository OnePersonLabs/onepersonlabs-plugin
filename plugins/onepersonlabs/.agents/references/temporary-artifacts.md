# Temporary artifacts and recovery

Before saving generated evidence, receipts, diagnostics, or backups, identify
who needs them, what keeps them valid, and who removes them when that need ends.
Return evidence in the conversation unless a concrete consumer needs it later.
Use the existing owner and mark disposal in a recognizable name, directory, or
existing record when sufficient. Avoid a separate archive or sidecar for each
document.

Delete disposable artifacts when their purpose ends, including on completion,
cancellation, supersession, or abandonment. Clean up immediately where possible
and sweep leftovers during an existing startup, refresh, or next operation.
Let a later sweep handle interrupted cleanup. Avoid a ledger, scheduler, or
crash protocol solely to guarantee deletion at an exact time.
Use the owning contract's retention window. Otherwise keep inactive temporary
diagnostics and verified rollback backups for seven days. Reading an artifact
does not reset that window.

Reconcile uncertain operations before deleting recovery state. Keep only the
evidence still needed for reconciliation. Retain qualification evidence while
its exact claim and version have a consumer. Preserve authored content and
unresolved human submissions. Report unresolved cleanup failures. A cleanup log
or archive does not dispose of the artifact.
